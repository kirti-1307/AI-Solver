Standard reaction thermodynamics

Pick a reaction or type your own. The elemental balance is checked before anything is calculated. Write phases as (g), (l), (s); if you leave the phase out, gas is assumed when available.

Effect of pressure at constant temperature

For an ideal gas, enthalpy does not depend on pressure. For a real gas it does, and the difference is the residual enthalpy HR. Here every reactant and product is taken as the pure species at T and P: ΔH(T,P) = ΔH°(T) + Σ νi HiR(T,P).

Build a reaction from other reactions (Hess's law)

Add two or more reactions, each with a multiplier. A negative multiplier reverses the reaction. The solver adds the stoichiometry, cancels the intermediates and gets ΔH, ΔS, ΔG and K of the net reaction two ways: by summing the steps and directly from the net equation.

Product slate: what actually forms? extra feature 1

A single-reaction ΔG tells you whether one reaction can go. A real reactor has several reactions competing for the same atoms. Here you only choose which species are allowed to exist; the solver minimises the total Gibbs energy subject to the element balances and returns the equilibrium mixture. No reaction list is needed. Removing a species shows what a selective catalyst that cannot make it would be limited to.

Species allowed at equilibrium and feed (mol). Tick a species to allow it; give it moles to feed it.

Electrochemical lens extra feature 2

The same ΔG and ΔH, read as voltages. Run reversibly in an electrochemical cell, a reaction exchanges electrical work equal to ΔG, so Erev = −ΔG/(nF) and the thermoneutral voltage is Etn = −ΔH/(nF). A spontaneous reaction is a fuel cell; its reverse is an electrolyser.

Sensible heat of one species

Heat needed to take a species from T1 to T2 at constant pressure with no phase change: Q = n R ∫ (Cp/R) dT.

Solver versus published research

Data: Smith, Van Ness & Abbott, Introduction to Chemical Engineering Thermodynamics: Table C.1 (gas heat capacities), C.2 (solids), C.3 (liquids), C.4 (formation properties at 298.15 K) and B.1 (critical constants). Hydrogen peroxide and acetic acid, which SVN does not fully cover, use the NIST Chemistry WebBook and are labelled wherever they appear. Standard state: pure species at 1 bar; gases as ideal gases. R = 8.314 J/(mol·K).

All calculations run in your browser. The solver is the Python package thermosolver, executed with Pyodide.
